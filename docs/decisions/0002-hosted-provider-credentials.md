# Credentials and data rights for a hosted pilot

**Status** Proposed. Owner decision pending.
**Research date** 2026-11-18
**Related work** [Commercial model #2](https://github.com/hrabosh/wos-mcp-bridge/issues/2), [hosted profiles #8](https://github.com/hrabosh/wos-mcp-bridge/issues/8), [operating telemetry #9](https://github.com/hrabosh/wos-mcp-bridge/issues/9) and [marketplace pilot #12](https://github.com/hrabosh/wos-mcp-bridge/issues/12).

## Options and recommendation

| | Customer-owned key (BYOK) | Operator-owned commercial key |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What the customer pays for | Managed deployment and support. The customer keeps its own WoS subscription and bears the upstream costs and quota. | Hosted access and support within an agreed allowance. The operator pays for the commercial agreement and owns upstream cost and quota. |
| Key and caller access | The customer or institution owns the registered Clarivate application and provisions and revokes its key. The host stores the key for one isolated customer instance and gives MCP access only to users covered by that entitlement. | The operator holds and rotates the key for its registered application. Customers authenticate to MCP and get only the collections, fields and tools covered by the commercial agreement and their plan. |
| Deployment and budget | One authenticated endpoint and one process per customer. Separate secrets, clients, caches and rate-limit state. Hosting cost grows with customer count. Budget below the applicable subscription limit, allowing for the customer's other use. | An isolated instance is possible for one pilot customer. Sharing an authorized key across customers requires an aggregate upstream budget and per-customer allowances even with separate instances. A later shared process also needs authenticated tenant identity and tenant-scoped access, cache and accounting. |

Prefer one dedicated BYOK instance for the first WoS pilot if the customer's terms permit the proposed host, AI client, users and processing, and the chosen platform can isolate and rotate secrets. Record the owner’s choice after checking those conditions. The operator-key option requires written terms expressly permitting paid access for external customers and the intended use of the data. Use the selected marketplace's billing and fees if a paid pilot proceeds.

If WoS permissions remain unresolved, the `literature` profile provides a pilot option with `search_literature`, `resolve_article`, `get_article` and `get_article_section`, subject to the literature rights below. It runs without a WoS account or key.

## Provider evidence and open permissions

- **Clarivate.** Starter publishes trial limits of 1 request/s and 50/day, institutional member limits of 5/s and 5,000/day, and institutional integration limits of 5/s and 20,000/day with administrative approval. The trial excludes times-cited counts. The bridge searches Core Collection (`db=WOS`) within the applicable entitlement and returns bibliographic records. Clarivate binds the key to the registered application. The Starter portal's exact [Terms of Use](https://clarivate.com/wp-content/uploads/dlm_uploads/2019/08/End-User-Terms.pdf) and [Product/Service Terms](https://clarivate.com/download/web-of-science-apis/) returned HTTP 403. A 2024 Clarivate agreement published in the Czech contract register addresses Starter in its API exhibit and requires express written permission for AI use under that institution's contract. The exhibit also restricts third-party integrations and redistribution. The applicable current order and addenda are still needed for either pilot model. [Starter description and plans](https://developer.clarivate.com/apis/wos-starter), [application keys](https://developer.clarivate.com/help/api-access), [public contract example](https://smlouvy.gov.cz/smlouva/soubor/37342372/Technical%20University%20Liberec-%20OF-WoS%20API%20Expanded%20Basic-%20No%20EasyRenew%20_Sep%2020%2C%202024.pdf).
- **Europe PMC.** Its public REST API supplies bibliographic data and available abstracts without an API key. The OA subset supplies full-text XML under article-specific terms. EMBL-EBI preserves third-party rights and expects attribution. A paid profile should return bibliographic identifiers and links where permitted and pass abstracts or sections to the MCP client only where rights cover caching, AI processing, commercial use and delivery for that copy. Preserve source, licence and required attribution. An OA flag or link identifies a potentially accessible copy. The article’s licence governs reuse. No published numeric REST quota or per-request charge was verified. [REST API](https://dev.europepmc.org/RestfulWebService), [OA subset](https://dev.europepmc.org/downloads/openaccess), [copyright](https://staging.europepmc.org/Copyright), [EMBL-EBI terms](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/about/terms-of-use/).
- **OpenAlex.** OpenAlex publishes its dataset under CC0. Linked article text follows the rights of its relevant version. The bridge uses DOI/PMID single-work lookups for metadata and links. These lookups are free under current published pricing and still subject to rate limits. OpenAlex lists 10 requests/s per IP without a key, 30/s for free or prepaid keys, and 50/s for annual plans. Daily budgets for metered operations start at USD 0.10 keyless and USD 1 with a free key. The service terms separately restrict allowing others to access or use a Premium subscription without permission. Verify the proposed account and service use before relying on a shared paid plan. [Data licence](https://help.openalex.org/data/how-its-built/), [pricing](https://help.openalex.org/access/example-costs/), [authentication](https://help.openalex.org/api/authentication/), [service terms](https://openalex.org/OpenAlex_termsofservice.pdf).
- **Bridge source code.** The Apache-2.0 licence covers the code. Provider access, bibliographic data and article text each depend on the relevant provider or rights-holder terms. [Repository licence](https://github.com/hrabosh/wos-mcp-bridge/blob/8bbd089/LICENSE).

Before selling hosted WoS functionality, record the applicable agreement, order or written permission for the selected model. Confirm which host and subprocessors can handle application credentials and metadata, which customer users and WoS collections and fields are covered, whether the named MCP/AI client may receive and process records, and what limits apply to client retention, training, exports and commercial redistribution. Record cache and log permissions, storage/deletion deadlines, key ownership, approved rotation/revocation procedure, and request/record caps and reset windows. For BYOK, confirm authority to provision an application key to the chosen host. For the operator model, confirm external customer entitlement and the commercial price and quota in writing. The owner can record a conditional preference while these permissions remain unresolved. Final selection and sale remain pending.

## Credentials, caching and accounting

Authenticate MCP callers and check tool entitlement before any cache read or upstream request. Inject `WOS_API_KEY` and optional `OPENALEX_API_KEY` using the instance's secret settings. MCP login tokens authenticate callers. API keys authenticate the server to its providers and stay out of tool arguments, responses and logs. Rotate by provisioning the replacement in the secret store, restarting the affected process to close its clients and clear its caches, and revoking the old key with the provider according to the approved procedure. Revoke exposed credentials immediately.

Provider clients, TTL caches and request-spacing/cooldown policies are per process. The default 15-minute TTL controls reuse. Expired entries may remain in memory until access, eviction or shutdown. Set `WOS_MCP_CACHE_TTL_SECONDS=0` until the applicable terms allow caching. Keep logs free of queries and records. Treat cached quota headers as historical. Shared keys or replicas require coordinated admission across processes, with a global provider limit and per-customer rate and usage caps. Aggregate keyless OpenAlex calls by egress IP and respect any shared Europe PMC capacity limit across instances.

## Unit economics

Count the actual requests after cache checks. Each explicit page can create another miss. Count attempted upstream calls separately where providers distinguish rejected from charged requests.

| MCP operation | WoS | Europe PMC | OpenAlex |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| All required cache lookups hit | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Uncached WoS search page or record fetch | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| Uncached literature search page | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| Uncached DOI/PMID resolution | 0 | 1 | Up to 1 single-work lookup |
| Uncached article or section read | 0 | 1 resolution + up to 1 eligible OA XML request | Up to 1 single-work lookup |

Example workload in one day, assuming fully warm or fully cold calls for simplicity. Of 100 WoS searches, 40 hit and 60 miss. Add 40 cold literature search pages and 20 article reads, of which five are fully warm and ten of the 15 cold reads request OA XML. This uses 60 WoS requests, 65 Europe PMC requests and up to 15 OpenAlex requests. The WoS trial's 50/day ceiling would leave at least ten of the required WoS misses outside the daily allowance. An eligible institutional or commercial plan still has to cover live usage and bursts. For conservative no-cache sizing, the same tool counts imply 100 WoS requests and up to 80 Europe PMC and 20 OpenAlex requests if every article read requires XML.

Over the billing period, contribution equals `R - F - H - U_wos - U_pmc - U_oa`. `R` is customer revenue, `F` is the marketplace fee from its actual schedule, and `H` is hosting, logging and support. Each `U` is the operator's invoice cost for that provider, including any fixed plan and overage charges. BYOK assigns the WoS invoice to the customer, so `U_wos` is zero in the bridge's accounts. For an operator key, use the actual quoted contract for `U_wos`. OpenAlex single-work requests have zero published marginal price. Include any selected OpenAlex plan and other confirmed provider charges. A cache hit still incurs bridge hosting and support costs.
